# Product Grilling

Reach shared understanding before shaping the product commitment. Treat the discussion as a **design tree**: each product decision can unlock dependent decisions.

## Work the decision frontier

1. Read the available evidence that can resolve the current material decision. Do not read every category or reopen settled choices.
2. Separate discoverable facts from choices. Research facts directly; ask the user only for decisions that change the outcome, scope, priority, acceptance, or release boundary.
3. Compute the current **frontier**: every unresolved decision whose prerequisites are already settled.
4. Ask one compact round covering that frontier. Number the questions and attach a recommended answer with its main trade-off to each.
5. Record accepted, changed, deferred, and contradicted decisions. Reopen downstream decisions when a premise changes.
6. Recompute the frontier and continue until the product manager can state the problem, target outcome, scope, non-goals, acceptance, and next action without guessing.

Do not ask a question whose answer depends on another open question in the same round. Do not turn a medium request into an exhaustive interview: stop when the remaining uncertainty is local, inexpensive, and reversible.

## Use product language

Challenge vague or overloaded terms and propose one canonical term. Test important rules with concrete customer and business scenarios. If the code or current product contradicts the user's description, surface the contradiction as a decision rather than silently selecting a side.

The output is a decision-ready product brief or an update to the existing authoritative product artifact. It does not implement the solution by itself.
